In practice: every number of points

The theorems certify separation at special sizes and, through the prefix lemma, a weaker floor for every N. In practice one stops wherever one likes. This page measures the worst separation over every prefix up to 223 points in dimension three, compares the residue sequences with the usual alternatives, and shows how to randomise them without losing the bound.

Measured values are exact separations of the plotted point sets (toroidal) and are illustrations, not theorems. Separation is not integration error; the paper does not test integration.

1

The worst prefix

Progressive rendering, stippling or adaptive sampling add points until some criterion says stop, so the number of points is not known in advance. The relevant quantity is the separation of every prefix, and the paper reports its worst value over a practical range,

𝒥 = min over 64 ≤ N ≤ 223 of sep(P[N]) = δ(P[N])·N1/3,

exact, in dimension three. It is easy to read: a sequence with 𝒥 = 0.25 never puts two of its first N points closer than a quarter of the grid spacing, for any N in the range. The curves below are drawn exactly from the prefix sizes at which δ drops (between drops, δ is constant and sep rises like N1/3).

3

Randomising without losing the bound

Randomised quasi-Monte Carlo needs many independent copies of a sampler. Owen scrambling redraws every digit below the stratum at random; it makes every (t, s)-sequence almost surely non-quasi-uniform (Suzuki) and would undo the constructions here. A digital shift adds one fixed random digit vector to every point, digit by digit modulo p. It keeps every certified bound (lem:shift): for the residue sequence the shifted prefix PsK−1 again occupies one coset of the lattice Λv, one point per fine cell, so the bound is the same. A uniformly random shift makes each point uniform and gives unbiased estimators.

The residue sequence of the table at p = 13. A shift must be additive in 𝔽p: a non-affine permutation of a geometric digit keeps the net property but can break the offset map (it brought the brick wall at p = 5 down to sep(P6) = 0.068, below its certified 0.2). The lemma keeps the certified bound, not the exact separation.

4

Limitations

A sequence in base p is stratified in base p, not in base 2: renderers that rely on dyadic strata at power-of-two sample counts should use base 2 or 4 (thm:B2, thm:K4, thm:S4). Within one base, sep(P[N]) fluctuates with the position of N between powers of p, more so for large p; for the residue sequences the smallest values come just after the certified sizes, when the first points of the next block arrive. A point costs ⌈logp N⌉ table lookups and additions per coordinate. The guarantees concern the geometry of the point sets: they matter where clumping at an unknown, open-ended number of samples must be excluded. Separation and integration error are different quantities, and a comparison on rendering integrands is the obvious next experiment.
